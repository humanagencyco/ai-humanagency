Café or restaurant

AI won't cook or serve, but it can take a lot of the writing and admin off your plate: menus, reviews, rotas and posts.

What it can help with

  • Write menu descriptions and specials-board wording
  • Reply to Google and Tripadvisor reviews in your own voice
  • Draft a weekly rota from who's free and when you're busy
  • Turn a photo and a few words into an Instagram or Facebook post
  • Write polite emails to suppliers, landlords or the council

Prompts: fill in the boxes, then copy or open

Specials board
Here are today's specials: [today's specials]. Write one short line for each for our chalkboard. Warm and simple, no more than 10 words each. Don't add ingredients I haven't listed.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
Reply to a review
Here's a Google review we got: [paste review]. Write a reply from me, [your name], the owner. Thank them and mention one thing they liked. Under 60 words. If it's a complaint, apologise once, don't argue, and invite them to email [your email].
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
Draft a rota
We're open [days and hours]. Lunch from 12 to 2 is our busiest time. Here's when each person can work: [first names and availability]. Draft next week's rota so there are always at least 2 people on at lunch. Show it as a table and flag any gaps.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
⚠️ Always check allergens and prices yourself. AI can get them wrong and sound sure about it. Use first names only for staff, and leave out phone numbers and addresses.

Shop

For a shop, AI is a fast writer and a patient analyst: product descriptions, customer questions, and making sense of your till exports.

What it can help with

  • Write product descriptions, shelf labels and window signs
  • Answer common customer questions for your website or WhatsApp
  • Spot your best and slowest sellers from a till or Shopify export
  • Plan a week of social posts in one go
  • Draft a simple returns or delivery policy in plain English

Prompts: fill in the boxes, then copy or open

Product description
Write a product description for our website. Product: [product name]. Facts: [size, material, colours, price, anything special]. About 60 words, friendly, for people who shop locally. Only use the facts I've given you.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
Best and slowest sellers
Here's last month's sales export: [paste your sales export]. Which 10 items sold best and which 10 barely sold? Show both in a table with units and takings, then suggest one thing to try for the slow ones.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
A week of posts
Plan 5 Instagram posts for next week for a [type of shop] in [town]. One a day, Monday to Friday. For each, give me a caption under 40 words and a simple photo idea I can take on my phone.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude

Trades

If you're on the tools all day, AI is most useful for the paperwork at the end of it: quotes, write-ups and replies, often from a voice note.

What it can help with

  • Turn rough notes or a voice note into a tidy quote or invoice
  • Write up a finished job with photos for the customer
  • Reply to enquiries fast, even when you're on a job
  • Explain a technical problem to a customer in plain English
  • Keep checklists for paperwork, certificates and renewals

Notes into a quote
Here are my notes from a job visit: [paste notes, or speak them into the app]. Turn them into a quote with a list of work, materials, labour and a total. My day rate is [£ day rate] and materials are at cost plus [markup %=10]%. Don't add any work I haven't mentioned.

Quick reply
A customer has messaged: [paste message]. I'm on a job until 4pm. Write a short, friendly WhatsApp reply saying I'll call after 4, and asking for their postcode and a photo of the problem.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
Explain it plainly
Explain to a customer, in plain English, why their [boiler / consumer unit / roof] needs [the work]. Two short paragraphs. No jargon and no scare tactics.

Office or services

For office work, AI is a first-draft machine and a speed reader. It's good at emails, summaries and explaining documents, as long as you check the result.

What it can help with

  • Summarise meetings and long documents (Copilot does this inside Microsoft 365)
  • Write the FAQs for your website
  • Explain and tidy up spreadsheets and formulas

Developers and technical people

AI coding tools now read your codebase, run commands and make changes. Used well, they're like a fast junior who never gets tired. You still own every line that ships.

What it can help with

  • Explain unfamiliar code, logs and stack traces
  • Find the likely cause of a bug and suggest the smallest fix
  • Write tests, scripts, docs and migration plans
  • Review a pull request for bugs, edge cases and security issues
  • Run multi-step tasks in your repo with agents like Claude Code, Codex or GitHub Copilot

Prompts: fill in the boxes, then copy or open

Debug an error
Here's a function and the error it throws: [paste code and stack trace]. Explain the most likely cause in 3 bullets, then suggest the smallest fix. Don't rewrite unrelated code, and tell me how to confirm the fix.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
Write tests
Write unit tests for this function using [pytest / Jest]. Cover normal cases, edge cases and bad input. List any behaviour that looks like a bug rather than writing a test that passes on it. [paste code]
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
Learn a codebase
I'm new to this repo. Trace how a request goes from [entry point] to [the database / external API]. List the files and functions in order, with one line on what each does. Point out anything that looks risky or surprising.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
⚠️ Review and test everything before it merges. Never paste secrets, keys or customer data. To go further, see Setup, Harness and Evals.

Project and programme managers

A lot of delivery work is turning messy inputs into clear outputs: notes into status reports, transcripts into actions, plans into risks. AI is very good at that first pass.

What it can help with

  • Turn weekly notes into a status report or steering pack
  • Pull decisions, actions and owners out of meeting transcripts
  • Stress-test a plan for risks, dependencies and assumptions
  • Rewrite one update for different audiences: execs, users, suppliers
  • Draft RAID logs, charters, lessons learned and cutover checklists

Prompts: fill in the boxes, then copy or open

Weekly status report
Here are my notes from this week: [paste your notes]. Write a one-page status update for the steering group with: overall RAG and a one-line reason, progress this week, next week, top 3 risks with owners, and decisions needed. Plain English. Don't invent dates or owners: mark anything missing as TBC.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
Actions from a meeting
Here's the meeting transcript: [paste the transcript]. List the decisions made, the actions with owner and due date, and the open questions. Only include what was actually said. If an action has no owner, say so.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
Stress-test the plan
Here's our plan summary: [paste milestones, dependencies, team]. Act as a sceptical programme director. List the 10 risks most likely to hurt us, with likelihood, impact and a mitigation for each, in a table. Then list the 3 assumptions I should check this week.
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
One update, three audiences
Rewrite this update three ways: for the CFO (cost, timing, risk in 5 lines), for end users (what changes for them and when), and for our supplier (what we need from them by when). [paste the update]
Open in ChatGPTOpen in Claude
⚠️ RAG status and judgement calls stay with you. AI doesn't know the politics, the history or which dates are real. Only paste programme material into a tool your organisation has approved.

Four habits that make any prompt work

Give it the factsPaste the details it needs. It can't see your shop, your notes or your inbox.
Treat it as a draftAsk for changes one at a time: "shorter", "friendlier", "drop the last point".
Check what mattersPrices, dates, names and rules. AI can sound sure and still be wrong.
Keep private things outNo passwords, card numbers or other people's personal details.
