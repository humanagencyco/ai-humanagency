Before you start

These prompts work in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. Not sure which to use? See Which AI tool?

  • Replace everything in [square brackets] with your own details.
  • The first answer is a draft. Ask for changes one at a time: “shorter”, “friendlier”, “drop the last point”.

  • Don't paste passwords, card numbers or other people's private details.

Café or restaurant

AI won't cook or serve, but it can take a lot of the writing and admin off your plate: menus, reviews, rotas and posts.

What it can do for you

  • Write menu descriptions and specials-board wording
  • Reply to Google and Tripadvisor reviews in your own voice
  • Draft a weekly rota from who's free and when you're busy
  • Turn a photo and a few words into an Instagram or Facebook post
  • Write polite emails to suppliers, landlords or the council

Prompts to try

Specials board

Here are today's specials: [list them]. Write one short line for each for our chalkboard. Warm and simple, no more than 10 words each. Don't add ingredients I haven't listed.

Reply to a review

Here's a Google review we got: [paste review]. Write a reply from me, [your name], the owner. Thank them and mention one thing they liked. Under 60 words. If it's a complaint, apologise once, don't argue, and invite them to email [your email].

Draft a rota

We're open [days and hours]. Lunch from 12 to 2 is our busiest time. Here's when each person can work: [first names and availability]. Draft next week's rota so there are always at least 2 people on at lunch. Show it as a table and flag any gaps.
Always check allergens and prices yourself. AI can get them wrong and sound sure about it. Use first names only for staff, and leave out phone numbers and addresses.

Shop

For a shop, AI is a fast writer and a patient analyst: product descriptions, customer questions, and making sense of your till exports.

What it can do for you

  • Write product descriptions, shelf labels and window signs
  • Answer common customer questions for your website or WhatsApp
  • Spot your best and slowest sellers from a till or Shopify export
  • Plan a week of social posts in one go
  • Draft a simple returns or delivery policy in plain English

Prompts to try

Product description

Write a product description for our website. Product: [name]. Facts: [size, material, colours, price, anything special]. About 60 words, friendly, for people who shop locally. Only use the facts I've given you.

Best and slowest sellers

Here's last month's sales export: [paste it, or attach the file]. Which 10 items sold best and which 10 barely sold? Show both in a table with units and takings, then suggest one thing to try for the slow ones.

A week of posts

Plan 5 Instagram posts for next week for a [type of shop] in [town]. One a day, Monday to Friday. For each, give me a caption under 40 words and a simple photo idea I can take on my phone.
Check any numbers against your till. AI can misread a spreadsheet, especially if columns are messy. Don't paste customer names, emails or card details.

Salon or beauty

Most salon admin is writing: reminders, aftercare, replies and captions. AI can draft all of it in your tone, so you spend less time on your phone between clients.

What it can do for you

  • Write booking reminder and follow-up messages
  • Turn your notes into a clear aftercare sheet for each treatment
  • Reply to reviews quickly and kindly
  • Write before-and-after captions for social
  • Tidy up your price list and treatment descriptions

Prompts to try

Booking reminder

Write a friendly text message reminding a client about their appointment. Include placeholders for name, treatment, day and time. Ask them to reply YES to confirm, and mention our [24-hour] cancellation policy. Under 300 characters.

Aftercare sheet

Here are my notes on aftercare for [treatment]: [paste your notes]. Turn them into a one-page aftercare sheet for clients. Short headings, bullet points, friendly tone. Only use what's in my notes, and end with 'Any questions, message us on [number].'

Captions

I've just done [describe the look] for a client. Write 3 Instagram caption options, each under 30 words, plus 5 hashtags for a salon in [town]. Warm and confident, not salesy.
Aftercare and reactions must come from you or the product maker's guidance, not from AI. Ask a client before you share their photo, and keep health notes out of AI tools.

Trades

If you're on the tools all day, AI is most useful for the paperwork at the end of it: quotes, write-ups and replies, often from a voice note.

What it can do for you

  • Turn rough notes or a voice note into a tidy quote or invoice
  • Write up a finished job with photos for the customer
  • Reply to enquiries fast, even when you're on a job
  • Explain a technical problem to a customer in plain English
  • Keep checklists for paperwork, certificates and renewals

Prompts to try

Notes into a quote

Here are my notes from a job visit: [paste notes, or speak them into the app]. Turn them into a quote with a list of work, materials, labour and a total. My day rate is [£] and materials are at cost plus [10]%. Don't add any work I haven't mentioned.

Quick reply

A customer has messaged: [paste message]. I'm on a job until 4pm. Write a short, friendly WhatsApp reply saying I'll call after 4, and asking for their postcode and a photo of the problem.

Explain it plainly

Explain to a customer, in plain English, why their [boiler / consumer unit / roof] needs [the work]. Two short paragraphs. No jargon and no scare tactics.
Regulations and safety are on you. AI is not a source for Building Regs, Gas Safe or wiring rules, so check the official guidance. Read every quote before it goes out.

Office or services

For office work, AI is a first-draft machine and a speed reader. It's good at emails, summaries and explaining documents, as long as you check the result.

What it can do for you

  • Draft emails, proposals and letters
  • Summarise meetings and long documents (Copilot does this inside Microsoft 365)
  • Write the FAQs for your website
  • Explain and tidy up spreadsheets and formulas
  • Turn a messy process into a simple checklist

Prompts to try

Reply to an email

Here's an email I received: [paste]. Write a reply that [agrees / says no politely / asks for more time]. Keep it under 120 words, friendly but professional, and match the tone of the original.

Summarise a document

Summarise the document below for someone who has 2 minutes. Give me: what it's about in one line, the 5 key points, any dates or deadlines, and anything I need to do. Only use what's in the document. [paste or attach]

Explain a spreadsheet

Here's a formula from our spreadsheet: [paste formula]. Explain what it does in plain English, step by step, and tell me if there's a simpler way to do the same thing.
Use the tool your work has approved for anything confidential, such as client files or contracts. If your company has Microsoft 365 Copilot, that's usually the one.

Developers and technical people

AI coding tools now read your codebase, run commands and make changes. Used well, they're like a fast junior who never gets tired. You still own every line that ships.

What it can do for you

  • Explain unfamiliar code, logs and stack traces
  • Find the likely cause of a bug and suggest the smallest fix
  • Write tests, scripts, docs and migration plans
  • Review a pull request for bugs, edge cases and security issues
  • Run multi-step tasks in your repo with agents like Claude Code, Codex or GitHub Copilot

Prompts to try

Debug an error

Here's a function and the error it throws: [paste code and stack trace]. Explain the most likely cause in 3 bullets, then suggest the smallest fix. Don't rewrite unrelated code, and tell me how to confirm the fix.

Write tests

Write unit tests for this function using [pytest / Jest]. Cover normal cases, edge cases and bad input. List any behaviour that looks like a bug rather than writing a test that passes on it. [paste code]

Learn a codebase

I'm new to this repo. Trace how a request goes from [entry point] to [the database / external API]. List the files and functions in order, with one line on what each does. Point out anything that looks risky or surprising.
Review and test everything before it merges. Never paste secrets, keys or customer data. To go further, see Setup, Harness and Evals.

Project and programme managers

A lot of delivery work is turning messy inputs into clear outputs: notes into status reports, transcripts into actions, plans into risks. AI is very good at that first pass.

What it can do for you

  • Turn weekly notes into a status report or steering pack
  • Pull decisions, actions and owners out of meeting transcripts
  • Stress-test a plan for risks, dependencies and assumptions
  • Rewrite one update for different audiences: execs, users, suppliers
  • Draft RAID logs, charters, lessons learned and cutover checklists

Prompts to try

Weekly status report

Here are my notes from this week: [paste]. Write a one-page status update for the steering group with: overall RAG and a one-line reason, progress this week, next week, top 3 risks with owners, and decisions needed. Plain English. Don't invent dates or owners: mark anything missing as TBC.

Actions from a meeting

Here's the meeting transcript: [paste]. List the decisions made, the actions with owner and due date, and the open questions. Only include what was actually said. If an action has no owner, say so.

Stress-test the plan

Here's our plan summary: [paste milestones, dependencies, team]. Act as a sceptical programme director. List the 10 risks most likely to hurt us, with likelihood, impact and a mitigation for each, in a table. Then list the 3 assumptions I should check this week.

One update, three audiences

Rewrite this update three ways: for the CFO (cost, timing, risk in 5 lines), for end users (what changes for them and when), and for our supplier (what we need from them by when). [paste update]
RAG status and judgement calls stay with you. AI doesn't know the politics, the history or which dates are real. Only paste programme material into a tool your organisation has approved.

IT directors and leaders

Leaders get most value from AI as a thinking partner and a fast drafter: policies, board papers, vendor reviews and deciding which ideas to back.

What it can do for you

  • Draft an AI acceptable use policy and guidance for staff
  • Score and prioritise AI ideas from across the business
  • Summarise vendor proposals, contracts and RFP responses
  • Draft board papers and investment cases, then challenge them
  • Prepare for difficult conversations with suppliers or the board

Prompts to try

AI use policy

Draft a one-page AI acceptable use policy for a [size] [sector] company in the UK. Cover: approved tools, data that must never be pasted in, when a human must review the output, and who to ask. Plain English, for all staff, not lawyers.

Prioritise AI ideas

Here are the AI ideas my teams have suggested: [list]. Score each from 1 to 5 on value, effort, risk and data readiness, in a table. Recommend 3 to pilot this quarter, say why, and say what would tell us each pilot worked.

Review a vendor proposal

Summarise this vendor proposal for a board member: what we get, what it costs over 3 years, lock-in and data risks, and the 5 questions we should ask before signing. [paste or attach]

Challenge my paper

Here's my draft board paper: [paste]. Act as a sceptical non-executive director. What are the 5 hardest questions I'll be asked, and where is my case weakest?
Accountability doesn't move to the tool. Check data protection duties (UK GDPR and ICO guidance) and your sector's rules before rolling anything out. See the Enterprise AI Framework for the bigger picture.
